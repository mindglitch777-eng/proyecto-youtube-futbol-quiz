'''Robert Lewandowski''' ({{IPA|pl|ˈrɔbɛrt lɛvanˈdɔfskʲi|-|Pl-Robert Lewandowski.ogg}}; born 21 August 1988) is a Polish professional [[Association football|footballer]] who plays as a [[Striker (association football)|striker]] for [[Major League Soccer]] club [[Chicago Fire FC|Chicago Fire]] and [[Captain (association football)|captains]] the [[Poland national football team|Poland national team]]. Widely regarded as one of the greatest strikers of all time, he is one of only five players to have scored 100 goals with three different clubs,{{refn|group=note|The other four players are [[Isidro Lángara]], [[Romário]], [[Neymar]] and [[Cristiano Ronaldo]].}} ranks third for the all-time [[European Cup and UEFA Champions League records and statistics#All-time top scorers|top goalscorers]] in the [[UEFA Champions League]] with 109 goals,<ref>{{cite web |title=All-time Player &#124; Top goalscorers Stats &#124; UEFA Champions League |url=https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/history/rankings/players/goals_scored/ |access-date=3 August 2025 |publisher=UEFA |archive-date=10 October 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251010221537/https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/history/rankings/players/goals_scored/ |url-status=live }}</ref> and ranks fourth for the [[List of men's footballers with 50 or more international goals|all-time European men's top goalscorers]] in international football (92). He has scored over [[List of footballers with 500 or more goals|750 senior career goals]] for club and country. In addition, he is also one of a select number of players who have [[List of men's footballers with 1,000 or more official appearances|amassed over 1,100 career appearances]] (the first Pole to achieve the feat, since the time matches are recorded).

Beginning his career in the third and second tiers of Polish football with [[Znicz Pruszków]], Lewandowski moved to top-flight [[Lech Poznań]], helping the team win the [[2009–10 Ekstraklasa]]. In 2010, he transferred to [[Borussia Dortmund]], where he won two consecutive [[Meisterschale|Bundesliga titles]] and the [[2013–14 Bundesliga#Top scorers|league's top goalscorer]] award. In 2013, he also featured with Dortmund in the [[2013 UEFA Champions League final]]. Ahead of the [[2014–15 Bundesliga|2014–15 season]], Lewandowski joined Dortmund's domestic rivals, [[FC Bayern Munich|Bayern Munich]], on a [[Free transfer (association football)|free transfer]].
